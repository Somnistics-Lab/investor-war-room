1 — What you're being asked to underwrite

Somnistics Research Labs is a Seattle-based applied-science company building Pausality, an in-moment autonomic regulation app delivered through the Apple Watch and iPhone. The product trains a measurable physiological skill — autonomic flexibility — in the seconds when a person needs it most: mid-case, mid-shift, mid-decision.

The investment instrument is a SAFE on a $5.5M post-money cap. The round target is $250K; $125K is committed; the remaining $125K is what we are closing now through a tight set of relationships. We are not raising broadly. We are selecting.

This document is the depth layer behind the deal mechanics on the main page. The main page tells you the what. This tells you the what for — and the honest answer to how you get paid back.

2 — The market in one page

Two distinct surfaces, sized differently.

Surface one — consumer and wellness

318,000 monthly Google searches for breathwork content, growing 17% YoY. The biofeedback device market is moving from $193.7M (2024) to a projected $312M by 2032. Consumer wellness spending in 2026 is approximately $60B in budgeted dollars. The category is dominated by Calm and Headspace — together 60–70% of meditation app share — but they retain only 4.7% of users after thirty days. Large, growing, structurally unable to retain users on the consumer-only model.

Surface two — clinical and enterprise (where SRL operates)

65,000 CRNAs. 4M licensed RNs. ~1M physicians. Clinician burnout is now a board-level priority at every health system in the country. Lyra covers 20M lives; Spring Health is at $3.3B; Grow Therapy raised $150M at $3B in March 2026. These are the enterprise mental health gatekeepers, and their model is structurally biased toward the 20% of employees who need therapy. The 80% who need physiological skill-building have no precision tool. That is the gap.

Why now — May 2026

Two events open the window simultaneously.

  • Detection has been solved. WHOOP is at $10.1B post-Series G. Oura confidentially filed for IPO this month with projected 2026 revenue of $1.5–2B. The Apple Watch is on 100M+ wrists. The autonomic signal is being captured continuously and inexpensively.
  • Intervention has not been solved. Detection produces dashboards and scores, not trained capacity. The May 2026 evidence base (Yoo, Ding, Grabo, Spalding) just matured enough to ground intervention claims at clinical-grade precision. The product space is now ready for an intervention category that is to detection what physical therapy is to fitness tracking.

4 — The four-layer ecosystem

SRL's operating plan is four sequential layers. Each layer funds the next, and each independently raises the floor under the company's valuation.

LAYER 1

Clinical credibility — the Library-Graph

The foundation. A standards-based knowledge graph (Dublin Core, SKOS, PROV-O) currently documenting 200+ canonical concepts and 100+ verified-DOI evidence notes. Every clinical claim traces to a primary source. Unusual in the wellness category. This is the artifact that distinguishes SRL from companies that look similar from a distance.

LAYER 2

Pausality — the validated consumer surface

iOS + Apple Watch. The unit of use is a 60-second NeuroMinute. Designed for in-moment deployment rather than pre-event preparation — a design choice the Spalding 2025 null finding has empirically supported from the opposite direction. The consumer surface generates validation data, recruits clinical cohorts, and eventually serves as the B2B2C delivery vehicle to enterprise employees.

LAYER 3

Enterprise pilot → enterprise contract

The revenue layer. CRNA-program pilot → hospital-system contract → multi-system rollout → self-insured-employer expansion. Per-seat annual contracts with clinical-outcome reporting. Apollo Neuro: $30.7M raised against 17 clinical studies. Spring Health: $3.3B valuation on enterprise contract base. SRL's pilot-to-contract conversion math determines venture-scale versus revenue-scale valuation.

LAYER 4

The clinical-LLM moat

Every enterprise contract generates clinical-outcome data tied to physiological measurement. Aggregated, this becomes the training corpus for a clinical autonomic-regulation language model. Detection companies — Oura, WHOOP, Samsung, Apple — cannot build this without partnering. They are sensors. SRL is the protocol-response engine that turns sensor data into clinical action. This is the layer that determines whether the eventual exit is a $50M acqui-hire or a $500M+ strategic acquisition.

Parallel track: the CE course. S.T.E.P. (Somnistics Training and Education Protocol) generates near-term cash flow against a known purchasing pattern — CE renewal is legally required for active license. This is not the company's main story; it is the financial bridge that lets the company stay disciplined on enterprise contract terms.

6 — How you get your money back

Three plausible paths to return-of-capital, ranked by likelihood and typical multiple. Each names what would have to be true.

PATH 1 · HIGHEST LIKELIHOOD · LARGE MULTIPLE

Strategic acquisition by a sensor platform

WHOOP and Oura are the obvious acquirers. Both have publicly stated intentions to IPO. Both face the same internal problem: detection without intervention is a stalled product narrative. Both have the cash and strategic motive to acquire the clinical protocol layer before going public.

Comparables: Sword Health acquired Kaia Health for $285M (2026). Apollo Neuro at $100M+ valuation with 17 clinical studies. Below-bound: Peloton acquired Breathwrk for $2.2M (0.29x return on $7.7M raised — explicitly not the profile we are building).

Math: At a $5.5M post-money cap, an acquisition in the $50–250M range produces a 9x–45x cash return on the cap, before subsequent-round dilution. The acquisition does not need to be a unicorn; it needs to clear the cap and dilution math.

What would have to be true: at least one CRNA-program pilot converts to a single-system enterprise contract, the clinical-validation dataset has reproducibility across the cohort, and the timing aligns with WHOOP's or Oura's pre-IPO M&A window (2026–2028).

PATH 2 · MODERATE LIKELIHOOD · MODERATE-TO-LARGE MULTIPLE

Strategic acquisition by an enterprise mental health platform

Lyra, Spring, Headspace Health, and Grow Therapy are all consolidating point solutions into bundled offerings. The pattern favors validated clinical solutions serving employee segments their core therapy product does not reach well.

Comparables: Spring Health–Alma (~10M visits combined). Lyra–Bend Health. Sword–Kaia ($285M). Multiples in this category cluster around 6–12x trailing revenue for clinically-validated point solutions, or fixed-price strategic acquisitions in the $50–500M range when the buyer is integrating a category.

What would have to be true: the enterprise contract pipeline matures to at least three signed system contracts, the clinical-outcome data is reproducible, and the buyer's strategic narrative requires a "physiological skill-building" line item in its bundle.

PATH 3 · MODERATE LIKELIHOOD · MODERATE MULTIPLE

Revenue-scale exit with founder retention

If enterprise contracts mature and CE course revenue compounds, the company reaches $1–10M ARR without significant additional capital. At that point, SRL is a candidate for revenue-scale acquisition by a non-platform acquirer (regional health system holdco, CE-focused PE firm, clinical content roll-up) at a 4–8x ARR multiple.

Math: the lower-variance path. 4x–15x returns on the F&F cap rather than the 9x–45x of Path 1, but the highest probability of producing some return.

What would have to be true: AANA and ANCC CE accreditations come through, consumer app reaches modest organic growth, at least one enterprise contract anchors recurring revenue narrative.

We are not telling you SRL is going to IPO. IPO is a marginal exit pathway in digital health right now — Rock Health's Q1 2026 data shows 43 M&A deals against a near-empty IPO calendar for non-platform companies. WHOOP and Oura can IPO because they are platforms with hundreds of millions in revenue. SRL's most credible exit is acquisition by a platform, not competition with it.

What we will not promise you

We will not promise you a 100x. We will not tell you the company is going to "be the next Calm." Calm's revenue dropped 24% in 2025; Headspace's $3B valuation compressed by 89%. The model that produced those valuations is structurally broken at retention. The companies that win the next cycle are the ones that earn clinical depth before they scale brand. That is the bet you are being asked to make.
